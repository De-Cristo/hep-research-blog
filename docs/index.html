HEP weekly reading for 4 November 2026

Week one · Reading notes and paper guides

Start here

This edition covers advances in precision calculations, flavour measurements, long-lived-particle searches, and analysis methods. The summaries explain what each paper delivers, how it obtains the result, and where its claims stop. Each has a short guide to the most useful evidence in the paper.

Precision calculations

Toponium production at NNLO

Klein and collaborators calculate the next-to-next-to-leading-order QCD correction to the short-distance production coefficient for pseudoscalar toponium. Their nonrelativistic QCD treatment separates hard production from the binding dynamics of a slowly moving top pair. It includes electroweak and relativistic corrections, additional production channels, and perturbative bound-state matrix elements.

At 13 TeV, the inclusive colour-singlet rate is 4.87 pb, with combined scale and parameter uncertainties of +0.63/−0.65 pb. Going from NLO to NNLO reduces renormalization- and factorization-scale uncertainties by about a factor of four, to a few percent. Uncertainty in the Coulomb scale of the bound-state matrix elements now dominates. The calculation assumes a zero-width top quark and sums bound states below threshold. Connecting this rate to the measured top-pair spectrum still requires a finite-width treatment.

Reading guide: The methodology explains the factorization; Equation 9 gives the combined prediction. The conclusion separates the improved hard coefficient from the finite-width extension still needed. Paper and full text.

Radiation vetoes in Higgs production

Becher and collaborators study the radiation veto between the two tagging jets in vector-boson-fusion Higgs production. This veto suppresses the QCD-induced Higgs-plus-two-jet background, but also produces logarithms that fixed-order predictions cannot fully describe. The paper combines leading-colour non-global logarithms, calculated with the Marzili shower, with super-leading logarithms from Glauber exchanges at the physical number of colours.

The two effects change signal and background differently. Non-global evolution strongly suppresses the background while leaving the VBF signal relatively unchanged. At a 25 GeV veto scale, the isolated super-leading contributions are about −1.3% for signal and +7.5% for background, relative to their Born rates for the stated scale choices. The authors compare additive and multiplicative combinations because full colour-correlated joint evolution remains unfinished. Their difference measures sensitivity to missing terms rather than providing a unique final prescription.

Reading guide: Figures 3–4 distinguish the separate corrections from their combination. Section 3.3 explains the assumptions behind the two prescriptions and their different background predictions. Paper and full text.

Positive event weights through moment matching

Assi, Poncelet, and Whitehead propose constructing positive-weight event samples by matching perturbative moments. The aim is to retain higher-order information without the statistical cancellations associated with negative event weights. The abstract checked in the earlier review reports applications to Drell–Yan and Z-plus-jet observables at NNLO. The accuracy claim depends on the chosen observables and the approximation used in matching; it should not be read as an unconditional guarantee for every distribution.

Reading guide and verification limit: The important question is which observables are constrained and how the matching approximation is controlled. Direct access to the paper remained unavailable for this revision, so this entry retains the earlier abstract-based summary without a section or figure guide. Paper record.

Polarization in energy correlators

Gao, Lee, and Zhang calculate energy correlators for boosted W and Z bosons decaying hadronically. In the narrow-width approximation, the laboratory spectrum is assembled from longitudinal and transverse decay spectra, weighted by polarized production rates. Analytic boost kernels connect the rest-frame correlators to the measured angular variable. The calculation combines NNLO ingredients with resummation in the collinear and back-to-back limits.

For high-transverse-momentum W/Z production, polarization changes the predicted spectrum by about 10% compared with an unpolarized treatment. A separate Monte Carlo example uses jets recoiling against a leptonic Z. Hadronic W/Z decays generate an angular feature of about 7%, although they account for only about 1% of that selected jet sample. No boson tag or jet-mass window is applied. This is a theoretical and simulation demonstration, not a detector-level measurement of polarization sensitivity.

Reading guide: Sections III–IV connect the boost kernels to polarized collider spectra. Section V and Figure 8 show the untagged angular feature and explain the limited event rate of that example. Paper and full text.

Experimental results

Evidence for the rare neutral B decay to two muons

LHCb measures B⁰ → μ⁺μ⁻ and Bₛ⁰ → μ⁺μ⁻ using 18 fb⁻¹ collected with its upgraded detector in 2024–2025. These highly suppressed decays provide sensitive tests of additional contributions to flavour-changing interactions. The analysis extracts signal yields from simultaneous dimuon-mass fits in classifier categories, then converts them to branching fractions using normalization decays. A separate decay-time analysis determines the Bₛ⁰ effective lifetime.

The B⁰ signal reaches 3.0 standard deviations in the new sample and 3.2 when combined with earlier LHCb data: the first single-experiment evidence for this decay. Its combined branching fraction is 1.14 × 10⁻¹⁰, with statistical uncertainty +0.42/−0.39 and systematic uncertainty +0.06/−0.05 in units of 10⁻¹⁰. The combined Bₛ⁰ branching fraction is 3.25 × 10⁻⁹, with statistical uncertainty +0.26/−0.25 and systematic uncertainty +0.13/−0.12 in units of 10⁻⁹. The combined effective lifetime is 1.60 ± 0.16 ± 0.01 ps. All results agree with the Standard Model; B⁰ remains evidence rather than observation.

Reading guide: The Results section separates Run 3 from combined measurements. Figure 5 shows the branching-fraction likelihood, and Figure 6 shows the Run 3 lifetime fit. Paper and full text.

Measuring Vcb through W decays in top events

CMS extracts |Vcb| from the rare W → cb decay in top-pair events, using 138 fb⁻¹ at 13 TeV. One W decays leptonically and the other hadronically. A multivariate reconstruction assigns jets to the top decays; a discriminant using flavour-tagging and kinematic information isolates the rare hadronic W decay. The fit combines a single-lepton signal region with a dilepton control region to constrain backgrounds with additional heavy-flavour jets.

The result is |Vcb| = 0.0459, with statistical uncertainty +0.0044/−0.0048 and systematic uncertainty +0.0064/−0.0076. Its precision is limited mainly by modelling of top production with additional heavy-flavour jets, alongside flavour-tagging uncertainties. It agrees with both inclusive and exclusive semileptonic B-decay determinations within its larger errors. The contribution is an independent measurement at the electroweak scale, without the nonperturbative QCD inputs required by those B-decay methods.

Reading guide: Figure 9 shows the |Vcb| likelihood. The Results and Summary sections explain the heavy-flavour background treatment and why this measurement does not yet resolve the B-decay discrepancy. Paper and full text.

Forward photon fusion and GeV scale axion-like particles

LHCb searches for axion-like particles produced and decaying through γγ → a → γγ in ultraperipheral PbPb collisions. The forward detector’s low-energy photon capabilities extend the search to masses of 3–10 GeV. The dataset is 0.228 nb⁻¹ at a nucleon–nucleon energy of 5.02 TeV. Relevant backgrounds include light-by-light scattering, gluon-induced diphotons, and electron pairs misidentified as photons.

No diphoton candidates survive the selection. In the model where the ALP couples only to photons, the 95% confidence-level coupling limits range from 3.2 × 10⁻⁴ to 9.5 × 10⁻⁴ GeV⁻¹. The improvement is strongest for photon-fusion production between 3 and 5 GeV. A 31% signal-normalization uncertainty accounts for differing acceptance predictions from the two generators used.

Reading guide: Sections 3–5 establish the selection, backgrounds, and systematic uncertainties. Figures 3–4 place the limits among existing constraints under the photon-only coupling assumption. Paper and full text, version 2.

Long-lived heavy neutral leptons beyond the tracker

CMS searches for W decays producing a prompt muon and a heavy neutral lepton, followed by a displaced decay containing two more muons. The displaced pair is reconstructed solely in the muon system. This targets decay distances where requiring tracker tracks would lose acceptance. The analysis combines 138 fb⁻¹ at 13 TeV with 34.6 fb⁻¹ at 13.6 TeV.

Observed yields agree with background predictions. Under the Majorana-HNL hypothesis, CMS sets 95% confidence-level limits across masses of 1–5 GeV. The approach is particularly useful around 2–4 GeV and small squared mixing values of order 10⁻⁶–10⁻⁵. Mixing changes both production and lifetime, so the excluded region cannot be obtained by simply rescaling a cross-section limit. The analysis reevaluates signal acceptance and distributions across the mass–mixing grid.

Reading guide: The Event reconstruction and Background estimation sections explain the distinctive experimental challenge. Figures 7–8 show the observed yields and their conversion into a Majorana-HNL exclusion. Paper and full text.

FASER constraints on a long-lived X17

FASER combines existing dark-photon and axion-like-particle searches to test a 17 MeV protophobic vector boson proposed as an explanation of the ATOMKI anomalies. Such a particle could travel approximately 480 metres from the interaction point and decay into an electron–positron pair inside FASER. The reinterpretation uses simulated production and parameterized detector efficiencies, with proton bremsstrahlung providing the main production contribution in the studied model.

The combined null searches exclude the previously available low-electron-coupling window, 6 × 10⁻⁵ < εₑ < 1.2 × 10⁻⁴, while a higher-coupling window remains. The statistical interpretation uses 90% confidence-level exclusions. This is a model-dependent reinterpretation of existing data, not a new signal or a general exclusion of every X17 explanation. Lifetime determines whether particles reach the detector and decay within it.

Reading guide: Section 2 specifies the coupling model. Section 6 and Figure 2 explain the exclusion and its dependence on neutron and electron couplings; Appendix A supplies detector-efficiency parameterizations. Paper and full text.

Methods and feasibility

Local information in calorimeter anomaly detection

Mendiratta, Weitz, and Duarte show how a whole-shower likelihood can hide a localized anomaly beneath fluctuations elsewhere in a calorimeter. Their method reuses CaloFlow’s latent representation. It combines squared latent coordinates within 14 geometric regions, standardizes the regional sums, and takes the largest score. Regional likelihood contributions perform similarly, suggesting that spatial aggregation drives the improvement.

For simulated 5 GeV neutral particles decaying to two photons at or just behind the calorimeter front face, regional scores exceed 95% signal efficiency. The global likelihood remains below 5% at the same 0.1% background efficiency. These headline comparisons use thresholds matched on test photons. The paper separately develops background-only conformal calibration with a finite-sample false-positive guarantee; that guarantee should not be attached to the matched-threshold comparison. The result remains specific to the simulated benchmarks and geometry.

Reading guide: Section 3 defines the scores and calibration. Table 1 gives the efficiency comparison; Figure 4 shows how the signal concentrates near the shower impact point. Paper and full text.

Compact classifiers built around decay structure

CoReNet builds a hypothesized decay tree into an event classifier. Shared networks assemble intermediate candidates and complete event hypotheses; learned weights combine possible jet assignments while respecting exchange symmetries. Training uses signal-versus-background labels, without truth-level pairing targets.

On fast-simulation samples of HH → bbbb against QCD multijets, a 1,508-parameter model achieves a test ROC AUC of 0.8664 ± 0.0031. Its performance is competitive with a much larger transformer, although the apparent advantage is not statistically significant across the tested folds. In HH → bbWW against semileptonic top pairs, including the background decay tree raises AUC from 0.8859 to 0.9004. SPANet remains higher at 0.9126 in that comparison. The paper demonstrates that explicit physics structure can make a compact classifier effective, not that a small model universally beats a transformer.

Reading guide: Tables 1–2 compare methods in both final states. Figure 5 and Tables 3–4 show the trade-off between network size and classification performance. Paper and full text.

Correcting parameter estimates for their interplay with PDFs

Zhu and collaborators address a fitting problem: parton distribution functions and physics parameters affect the same cross sections, so treating one as fixed can bias the other. Their semiparametric method starts from separate estimates, then applies an efficient-influence-function correction to produce a debiased one-step estimator. It aims to account for the interplay without repeatedly refitting PDFs or performing a costly joint fit.

The demonstrations use deep-inelastic-scattering pseudodata inspired by BCDMS and HERA. Repeated simulations show reduced estimator bias and confidence-interval coverage consistent with the intended 95% level. The HERA example uses 1,000 repetitions and a specified injected Wilson coefficient. These are controlled tests: other PDF contributions are fixed, the fitted distribution is constrained outside the measured x range, and the representation uses only eight basis functions. A realistic global PDF fit remains future work.

Reading guide: Section 3 develops the correction. Figures 11–13 compare bias and uncertainty calibration with separate and joint fits; Section 6 sets out the remaining global-fit limitations. Paper and full text.

Displaced dielectrons in top events

This phenomenological study considers long-lived axion-like particles produced with top pairs and decaying into displaced electrons. Prompt top-decay objects provide selection handles independent of the displaced decay, including for ALP masses below the dimuon threshold. The study spans masses of 17 MeV–10 GeV and proper decay lengths of 0.1 mm–1 m. Isolation, momentum, and trajectory-alignment requirements suppress simulated backgrounds.

For 13 TeV collisions and 500 fb⁻¹, the most sensitive region reaches projected limits of order 0.001 pb on production cross section times dielectron branching fraction. These refer to generated fiducial phase space with ALP transverse momentum above 20 GeV and electron |η| below 3.5. The baseline assumes 100% trigger, top-system reconstruction, and displaced-dielectron reconstruction efficiency. Instrumental backgrounds, magnetic curvature in the trajectory treatment, and losses from resolving collimated electrons are omitted. The result is an idealized generator-level projection, not a full-detector expected exclusion.

Reading guide: Table 2 defines the discriminating variables, and Figure 6 shows the signal efficiency after selection. Section 4 and Figure 8 connect the selection to projected limits and their assumptions. Paper and full text.
